# Code of Conduct

**English** | [Deutsch](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.de.md)

**Context:** rules for working together on this project | **Input:** contributions, reports and discussions | **Output:** what is expected, what is not tolerated and how incidents are handled

## What this is about

**Context:** who is welcome | **Input:** none | **Output:** the principle

This project is open to everyone who wants to contribute. Nobody shall be treated badly because
of origin, age, gender, disability, religion, sexual orientation, appearance, education or
experience.

## What is expected

**Context:** expected behaviour | **Input:** none | **Output:** five rules

* **Stay factual.** This is about legal texts and code, not about people. Whoever finds an error
  names the error.
* **Assume good intent.** Whoever reports a wrong classification wants to improve the tool, even
  if the tone is short.
* **Tolerate legal uncertainty.** Views on legal questions differ. That lies in the matter, not
  in the other person failing to understand.
* **Write clearly.** Explain technical terms, write out abbreviations. Nobody should feel stupid
  for not knowing an abbreviation.
* **Accept criticism.** Whoever submits a change gets questions. That is the purpose of review.

## What is not acceptable

**Context:** unacceptable behaviour | **Input:** none | **Output:** the list

* Insults, disparagement, personal attacks.
* Sexualised remarks and unwanted advances.
* Harassment, in public or in private.
* Publishing other people's private information without their permission.
* Deliberate disruption of the collaboration.
* Exposing someone for lacking expertise.

## Where this applies

**Context:** scope | **Input:** none | **Output:** the places covered

In everything that belongs to this project: issues, pull requests, discussions, descriptions of
changes. And wherever someone represents this project.

## If something happens

**Context:** reporting and enforcement | **Input:** an incident | **Output:** a decision and its possible consequences

Report to **dhuette@gmx.net**. The report is treated confidentially.

What happens then: receipt is confirmed. The incident is examined and, where needed, the people
involved are heard. Then a decision follows, and the reporter is told about it.

Possible consequences, depending on severity: a note, a public reprimand, removal of a
contribution, exclusion from a discussion, or a permanent ban.

This project is run by one person. That means the report goes to Daniel Hütte, and he decides.
If the report concerns him, there is no second instance here; in that case, use GitHub's own
reporting channels.
